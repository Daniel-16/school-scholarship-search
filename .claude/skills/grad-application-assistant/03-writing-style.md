# Writing Style

How everything written for this applicant should sound. Applies to statements,
essays, emails to supervisors, and form answers.

## Voice

First person, past tense for what was done, present for what is believed, plain
declarative sentences. British or American spelling consistently per target —
UK/Commonwealth institutions get British, US institutions get American, and
mixing them inside one document is the kind of carelessness a reader notices.

Write the way a competent engineer explains their work to a smart colleague in
another field. Not the way a prospectus is written.

## Rules

**Specific over impressive.** "Reduced order processing time by 30%" beats
"significantly improved operational efficiency". Every adjective should be
earning its place or be cut.

**Evidence immediately after the claim.** A sentence asserting a capability is
followed by the sentence that proves it, not by another assertion.

**No lifelong passion.** "I have been fascinated by computers since
childhood", "From a young age", "I have always been passionate about" — these
open a large fraction of every application pile and signal that nothing
specific is coming. Open on something that happened, or something you think.

**No empty superlatives about the institution.** "Your world-renowned
department", "the prestigious X programme" tells the reader you could not name
anything concrete. Name a lab, a module, a method, a person.

**Explain the technical work to a non-specialist on the committee.** A
graduate admissions panel usually includes someone outside your subfield. One
clause of plain-language context per technical claim.

**Short paragraphs.** Three to five sentences. A dense page does not get read
carefully at application 180 of 400.

**Contractions: no** in statements and proposals; **fine** in a short
supervisor email, where stiffness reads worse than informality.

## Banned constructions

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "I am writing to express my strong interest in…" | the actual first sentence of your argument |
| "I believe I would be a great fit" | the reason, and let the reader conclude it |
| "various projects" / "numerous technologies" | name them |
| "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art", "revolutionary" | what it actually does |
| "delve into", "embark upon", "in today's world" | nothing; delete the clause |
| "I am confident that" | the claim on its own |
| "passionate about" | what you did about it |

## Length discipline

Word and page limits are enforced by the portal, not by taste. A statement
over the limit is truncated mid-sentence or rejected on upload.

- Count the words of the **rendered text**, not the source file. Extract with
  `pdftotext` and count.
- Target 95–100% of the limit. Well under it reads as having little to say.
- When a limit is stated in pages, assume ~500 words per page at 11–12pt
  single-spaced unless the format is specified.

## Handling weakness

Never apologise, never over-explain, never volunteer a weakness the form did
not ask about. Where a form *does* offer a space for additional context
(most US applications do), use it once, factually, without self-deprecation:
state the circumstance, state what changed, stop. Two sentences.

## The test

Read the draft and ask: could another applicant to this programme have written
this sentence? If yes for most of the document, it is not finished.
