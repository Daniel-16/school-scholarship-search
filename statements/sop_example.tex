% --- 1. The hook: a specific technical problem, stated as a problem. -------
% Not "I have always been passionate about AI". The first sentence has to be
% one only this applicant could write.
In the drone system I built for my undergraduate thesis, the language model
was never the hard part. Translating ``survey the eastern perimeter and flag
anything unusual'' into a flight plan took a weekend. What took the rest of
the year was the layer underneath it: a deterministic validator that sat
between the generated plan and the flight controller and refused any command
that left the geofence, exceeded the battery envelope, or could not be
reversed. The model proposed; something that could not hallucinate decided.

% --- 2. What was actually built, and what it showed. ----------------------
% Evidence immediately after the claim. A committee member outside the
% subfield must be able to follow this paragraph.
That system, AutoBee, ran on ROS~2 against a physical aircraft, with a
computer-vision pipeline for crowd density estimation feeding its anomaly
detection. It worked, within limits I can state precisely: the validator
rejected unsafe plans reliably, but it could only check properties I had
thought to encode, and I had no principled way to know what I had missed.
That gap --- between a safety layer that is correct and one that is complete
--- is the reason I am applying for this programme rather than taking
another engineering role.

% --- 3. Why this is not a sudden interest: the thread through the record. --
I have since met the same problem twice more in production. At MathWallet I
built a command-line interface through which autonomous agents execute
cross-chain transactions for a wallet serving over two million users, where
the actuator is an irreversible financial transfer and the cost of an
unchecked plan is somebody's money. In Alris, an open-source browser agent I
maintain, I constrained the action interface itself so that the space of
things the model can do is small enough to reason about. Three systems, three
actuators --- a rotor, a ledger, a browser --- and one unsolved question
about how to bound what a language model is permitted to cause.

% --- 4. Why THIS programme. Named people, named work. ---------------------
% The paragraph that most applications get wrong. Name a group, a method or
% a module and say what you would do with it. Never the ranking.
[Programme-specific paragraph. Name one or two faculty members and the
specific work of theirs that connects --- not their general area --- and say
what you would want to do with it. Name the modules or research facilities
that bear on the problem above. If the department has a group working on
verification of learned policies, formal methods for autonomy, or agent
evaluation, that is the paragraph's anchor.]

% --- 5. What comes after. Concrete, and consistent with the funder. -------
% For a development-focused scholarship this paragraph carries real weight
% and must address return and impact explicitly.
After the MSc I intend to continue to a doctorate on runtime verification for
language-model agents, and then to work on these systems where they are
actually going to be deployed. Autonomous aerial survey is not a hypothetical
application in Nigeria --- it is already being used for pipeline inspection
and agricultural monitoring --- and it is being deployed by teams who have no
verification layer at all. I would rather be one of the people who builds
that layer than one of the people who writes about why it was missing.

% --- 6. The close: short, and not a summary. ------------------------------
I am not applying because I want to learn to build these systems. I have
built three. I am applying because I have reached the edge of what I can work
out on my own, and this is where the people who have gone further are.
